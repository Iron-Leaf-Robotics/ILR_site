The problem

Most field problems are found on foot, and found late.

Scouting a block means walking it. That takes time growers don't have in season, and what gets noticed depends on who walked it and when.

Found late

Gopher mounds and ground damage are easy to miss until the trees or vines above them are already affected.

Hard to find again

A problem spotted mid-row is rarely tied to a precise location, so follow-up means walking the row again.

Hard to compare

Without a consistent record, it's difficult to tell whether a block is getting better or worse over a season.

The platform

A sensor cart that records the whole row.

An operator walks the cart down the row. It captures both sides of the ground and canopy, and every measurement is stamped with when and where it was taken.

Navigation, colour and near-infrared cameras

Continuous video for coverage, plus high-resolution colour and NIR bursts at each stop.

Lidar, GPS and IMU

Shape and location data so each finding maps back to a specific spot in the block.

One clock for every sensor

All imagery is timestamped, so cameras, lidar and position line up after the fact.

Runs from a phone

A web-based operator app on the cart's network, with LTE for getting data out of the field.

  1. Walk the row

    The operator pushes the cart through the block at walking pace.

  2. Capture

    Cameras, lidar and position data record continuously, with detail shots at each stop.

  3. Review

    Candidate findings, like gopher mounds, are confirmed or rejected by a person before they count.

  4. Report

    Confirmed findings go back to the grower by row and location.

About

Built in the Okanagan, for the Okanagan first.

Iron Leaf Robotics was founded in Kelowna by Ryan Collins, an electrical and controls EIT with a background in industrial robotics, automation and aerospace engineering.

We design and build the cart in-house, and test it on local orchards and vineyards. That keeps iteration fast and keeps the work tied to problems growers actually have.

Our approach is to understand why the established answer exists before replacing it, and to only count what the field data supports.
